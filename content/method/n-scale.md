The N0–N4 scale is an ordinal coding device for the degree of legal embedding of a technology in one regulatory function. It is not a maturity index, it is never summed across layers, and a restrictive regime can score low precisely because the score measures integration rather than regulatory intensity.

Three rules follow. Scores are ordinal: the step from N1 to N2 is not assumed equal to the step from N3 to N4. Normalisation is non-linear and reversible: an incident, political change or new evidence may move a layer back toward conditional treatment. The range across the seven layers is a descriptive signal of inter-layer asymmetry, not a statistical index.

Draft law is excluded from current-law scores and shown separately as sensitivity or prospective rows.
